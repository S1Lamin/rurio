# BUSINESS MODEL

> # **BUSINESS MODEL**

> ## **San Xoán Gamified Rural Experience & Circular Tourism**

> ### **1\. Executive Summary**

**We propose a gamified rural experience business based in San Xoán de Río, Galicia, built around the assets already available to a local entrepreneur: a traditional stone house, 2 hectares of rural land, a car, strong local knowledge, digital skills, and up to €25,000 in available financing.**

**Instead of creating a conventional tourism business, we transform San Xoán itself into a living playground.**

**Visitors, families, Spanish tourists, companies, schools and local residents can participate in challenges, discover the territory, experience local culture and nature, interact with local businesses and earn rewards.**

**The business combines:**

* **Gamified tourism**  
* **Rural experiences**  
* **Corporate team-building**  
* **Local products and businesses**  
* **Sustainable tourism**  
* **Circular-economy activities**  
* **Digital gamification**  
* **Rural accommodation/experience hub**

**The objective is to build a business capable of generating more than €100,000 in revenue within the first 12 months, while starting with limited capital and progressively reinvesting revenues.**

> **We don't want people to simply visit San Xoán. We want them to play it, experience it, support it and leave a positive impact.**

**↓**

**Correct collection**

**↓**

**Validated local processing / bioconversion**

**↓**

**Potential agricultural input**

**↓**

**↓**

**↓**

# Financial Plan & Pricing Model

# **Financial Plan & Pricing Model**

## **1\. Financial Strategy**

Rurio follows a simple principle:

> **The free version gets people into the ecosystem. Rurio makes money when value is created.**

Tourists can discover San Xoán, participate in basic gamified activities and explore local businesses for free.

Rurio generates revenue when users book experiences, purchase local products or choose premium features. Local businesses can also subscribe to paid tools that help them attract tourists and manage their presence on the platform.

This creates an **asset-light, diversified and performance-based business model**.

&nbsp;

**2\. Rurio Pricing**

| Product / Service | Price | Rurio Revenue |
| ----- | ----: | ----- |
| Basic Rurio App | Free | User acquisition |
| Rurio Explorer Pass | €9.90 | 100% of pass revenue |
| Rurio Premium Pass | €19.90 | 100% of pass revenue |
| Experience bookings | Variable | 15% commission |
| Local marketplace | Variable | 10% commission |
| Business Basic | €19/month | Recurring revenue |
| Business Pro | €49/month | Recurring revenue |
| Rurio Box | €39–€79 | Product/fulfillment margin |

The free tier is essential: it reduces the barrier for tourists and creates the user base from which transaction revenue is generated.

**3\. How Rurio Generates €100,000**

Our pilot target is **€100,000 in annual Rurio revenue**.

We do not assume that €100,000 comes from one product. It is generated through four complementary revenue streams.

| ----- | ----: |

**4\. Revenue Stream 1 — Experience Bookings**

Local businesses and experience providers list activities on Rurio.

Examples include:

* Farm and food experiences

* Local gastronomy

* Craft workshops

* Nature activities

* Cultural experiences

* Guided routes

* Traditional activities

Rurio takes a **15% commission** on every booking.

### **Revenue target**

We target approximately **€266,700 in experience booking value (GMV)**.

At a 15% commission:

**€266,700 × 15% ≈ €40,000 Rurio revenue**

For example, if an experience costs €50:

* €42.50 goes to the provider

* €7.50 goes to Rurio

Rurio therefore earns more when local providers generate more business.

### **How we generate the bookings**

We will acquire tourists through:

* Partnerships with rural accommodation providers

* Tourism offices and destination organizations

* Local businesses

* QR codes at tourist touchpoints

* Social media and short-form content

* Local events and festivals

* Referral and reward mechanisms

* Gamified challenges

The objective is to turn existing tourism traffic into **measurable bookings**.

# RuRio Marketing Plan

# **RuRio Marketing Plan**

## **Executive Summary**

RuRio is a gamified rural experience platform launched in San Xoán de Río, Galicia, transforming a village and its surrounding landscape into an interactive playground for tourists, families, and corporate teams. The business combines rural tourism, local commerce, sustainability challenges, and digital gamification into a single destination experience.

This marketing plan covers the 12-month pilot period, aligning with RuRio's financial target of **€100,000 in annual revenue**. The marketing budget of **€4,000** (drawn from the initial €25,000 capital allocation) will be deployed to build brand awareness, acquire the first paying tourists, and sign up local business partners.

The strategy rests on three pillars:

1. **Partnerships first** — reach tourists through accommodation providers, tourism offices, and local businesses rather than expensive paid advertising.  
2. **B2B as revenue anchor** — corporate team-building packages (€2,500–€5,000/group) require fewer customers but generate the majority of early revenue, de-risking the seasonal B2C dependency.  
3. **Content-led organic growth** — short-form video and social content showcasing the San Xoán experience will drive inbound interest at near-zero cost, compounding over time.

Key 12-month targets: 2,500+ active Rurio users, \~100 paying local business subscribers, 15 corporate events booked, 200+ tourist experience bookings, and €100,000 in platform revenue.

## **Market Analysis**

### **Key Trends**

**Rural tourism is growing.** Post-pandemic travel behavior has shifted meaningfully toward rural, nature-based, and off-the-beaten-path experiences. Spain’s rural tourism sector recorded consistent growth through 2023–2025, with Galicia positioned as one of the top inland destinations due to its landscapes, Camino de Santiago traffic, and cultural identity.

**Gamification is mainstream.** Escape rooms, geocaching, scavenger hunts, and game-based experiences have moved from novelty to mainstream. Consumers, especially millennials and Gen Z, expect experiences to be interactive rather than passive. Corporate HR teams are actively seeking team-building formats that go beyond conference rooms.

**Sustainability is a purchase driver.** Conscious tourism is no longer a niche. Travelers increasingly select destinations and experiences based on environmental and social impact. Certification and impact tracking (like RuRio’s Green Impact Points) are becoming differentiators, not just marketing copy.

**Local commerce integration.** Tourists are spending more on local experiences and local products relative to international or chain alternatives. Platforms that connect visitors directly to local producers and artisans tap into a behavior that is already growing.

### **Competitive Landscape**

| Competitor type | Examples | Gap RuRio fills |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Rural accommodation platforms | Airbnb, Rusticae | No gamification or local ecosystem integration |
| Outdoor game experiences | Geocaching, local escape rooms | Not rural-rooted or community-linked |
| Corporate event agencies | Generic team-building providers | No place-based identity or sustainability layer |
| Destination apps | Local tourism office apps | Static, no gamification or local commerce |
| Local product marketplaces | Etsy, local markets | No experiential or tourism context |

**RuRio’s moat** is not the technology alone — it is the combination of deep local embeddedness, gamification, and circular economy integration that national or generic platforms cannot easily replicate in San Xoán.

### **Market Opportunity**

Galicia received approximately 4.5 million overnight domestic tourists in 2024\. The Ourense province (where San Xoán sits) is an underexplored corridor with growing interest. Corporate team-building in Spain is a €600M+ market with strong demand for differentiated outdoor formats. Even capturing a fraction of existing Galician tourism flows represents a viable path to €100K in year one.

## **Target Audience**

RuRio serves four distinct segments, each requiring a tailored marketing approach.

### **Segment 1 — The Experience-Hungry Tourist (B2C, Primary)**

**Who:** Spanish domestic tourists and Galician day-trippers aged 25–45. Likely urban professionals from Vigo, A Coruña, Pontevedra, or further afield (Madrid, Bilbao). Traveling with partners, friends, or small families.

**Needs:** A break from predictable tourism. They want to feel like explorers, not passengers. They care about local culture, food authenticity, and sharing an unusual story on social media.

**Buying behavior:** Discovers via Instagram/TikTok reels or travel blogs. Decides within 48 hours. Price-sensitive at the individual level (prefers free entry, will pay for experiences once engaged). Likely to book through a mobile-first interface.

**Key message:** *“Don’t visit San Xoán. Play it.”*

### **Segment 2 — The Corporate Event Buyer (B2B, Revenue Priority)**

**Who:** HR managers, office managers, and corporate event planners at mid-size Spanish companies (50–500 employees), primarily in Galicia and northern Spain. Budget holders with approval authority for €3,000–€10,000 team events.

**Needs:** A fresh, memorable format that their team will actually enjoy. Outdoor and nature-based. Differentiable from a generic karting day or restaurant dinner. Demonstrates company values (sustainability is a plus). Simple logistics.

**Buying behavior:** Discovers via LinkedIn, referral from other HR contacts, Google search (“team building Galicia”, “actividades empresa Galicia”). Long research cycle (2–4 weeks). Requires a professional proposal or deck. Wants references.

**Key message:** *“Turn your team’s next event into a story they’ll still be talking about.”*

### **Segment 3 — Local Business Partners (B2B2C, Platform Growth)**

**Who:** Restaurants, rural accommodation providers, farmers, artisans, and activity providers in the San Xoán and wider Ourense area. Small businesses, often owner-operated, with limited digital presence.

**Needs:** More customers. A simple digital tool they can actually use. Proof that it works before committing to a subscription.

**Buying behavior:** Responds to direct outreach and word-of-mouth from other local businesses. Decides quickly when shown tangible results. Sensitive to monthly fees — needs to see ROI within 60 days.

**Key message:** *“Get discovered by every tourist who arrives in San Xoán.”*

### **Segment 4 — Schools and Educational Groups (B2B, Secondary)**

**Who:** Primary and secondary school teachers and university faculty in Galicia planning field trips, sustainability workshops, or rural educational experiences.

**Needs:** A structured, curriculum-linked activity. Safety and clear itinerary. Affordable per-student pricing. Documentation for school administration.

**Buying behavior:** Slow cycle (1–3 months), committee approval required. Responds to clear program descriptions and references from other schools.

## **Strategies and Tactics**

### **Channel 1 — Partnership Distribution (Priority: High)**

The fastest and most cost-effective way to reach tourists is to go where they already are.

**Tactics:**

* Approach every rural accommodation provider (casas rurales, campsites, small hotels) within a 30 km radius. Offer free QR signage kit \+ RuRio listing in exchange for recommending the platform to guests.  
* Partner with Galicia’s official tourism offices (Xunta de Galicia, Turismo de Galicia) and Ourense municipal tourism to feature RuRio in their visitor materials.  
* Place QR codes at high-traffic tourist touchpoints: Camino de Santiago waypoints, viewpoints (miradores), parking areas for hiking trails, and local cafes.  
* Identify 2–3 Galician travel blogs or Instagram accounts (10K–100K followers, rural/slow travel focus) for barter or low-cost partnerships.

**Cost:** Primarily time; printed QR signage kits budget €300–€500.

### **Channel 2 — Social Media — Short-Form Video (Priority: High)**

Instagram Reels and TikTok are the primary organic discovery channels for experience-based tourism among 25–45-year-olds.

**Tactics:**

* Publish **3–4 short videos per week** during the first 6 months. Content themes: “Hidden San Xoán” (discover local secrets), challenge reveals, behind-the-scenes with local farmers/artisans, tourist reactions, sustainability stories.  
* Use the entrepreneur’s face and local personality as the brand voice — authenticity outperforms polished production at this stage.  
* Build a content calendar around Galician seasons, festivals (San Xoán itself, summer festivals, harvest season), and challenge launches.  
* Run one **hashtag challenge** per quarter to generate user-created content (e.g., \#JogaRuRio for participants to share their challenge completions).  
* Allocate **€500–€800 in Meta/TikTok boosting** per quarter to amplify top-performing organic posts, targeted at Spanish users interested in rural travel and team activities.

**Cost:** €1,000/year in paid boosting; content is produced in-house.

### **Channel 3 — LinkedIn B2B Outreach (Priority: High for Corporate Revenue)**

**Tactics:**

* Build a RuRio LinkedIn company page focused on corporate team-building content.  
* Identify and directly connect with **HR managers, People & Culture leads, and office managers** at mid-size companies in Galicia, Asturias, and greater northern Spain.  
* Share case study content from the first corporate events (photos, participant quotes, measurable outcomes).  
* Reach out with personalized direct messages offering a **complimentary planning call** for companies interested in outdoor team-building in 2026\.  
* Create a simple **one-page corporate PDF deck** for email follow-ups: what RuRio offers, pricing tiers, past clients, and sustainability credentials.

**Cost:** €0 in media spend; time investment is the primary cost.

### **Channel 4 — Local Business Onboarding Campaign (Priority: High for Subscriptions)**

**Tactics:**

* Personal in-person visits to the first 20 target local businesses. Demo the platform on a tablet or phone. Show how a profile looks and how bookings flow.  
* Offer a **30-day free trial of Business Pro** for founding partners who sign up in Month 1\.  
* Create a “Founding Partner” badge visible on RuRio profiles to incentivize early adoption and community belonging.  
* Host a monthly **local business breakfast** (informal, free coffee) to share platform updates, gather feedback, and strengthen the partner community.

**Cost:** In-person time \+ €200–€300 for partner materials and event refreshments.

### **Channel 5 — Content Marketing and SEO (Priority: Medium, builds over time)**

**Tactics:**

* Launch a simple RuRio blog or “Guide to San Xoán” section on the website. Topics: “Best local food experiences in Ourense”, “Sustainable travel in Galicia”, “Team building ideas in rural Spain”.  
* Target long-tail search terms with low competition and high buyer intent: “actividades team building Galicia”, “turismo rural Ourense experiencias”.  
* SEO is a slow burn — begin in Month 1 but expect meaningful organic traffic from Month 5+.

**Cost:** €0 if written in-house; €300–€500 if outsourced to a freelance copywriter.

### **Channel 6 — Email and WhatsApp Nurture (Priority: Medium)**

**Tactics:**

* Collect email/WhatsApp opt-ins at every touchpoint (QR landing pages, event sign-ups, business partner onboarding).  
* Send a **monthly newsletter** to tourist users: new challenges, seasonal events, local product launches, Green Impact leaderboard.  
* Use **WhatsApp Business** for corporate lead follow-up and for real-time guest communication during experiences (low-cost, high-trust channel in Spain).

**Cost:** €0 for WhatsApp; €150/year for a basic email platform (Mailchimp or equivalent).

### **Allocation Principles**

**Organic before paid.** The majority of tourist reach will come through partnerships and organic social content — paid social is an amplifier, not the foundation.

**Front-load partnerships.** The highest-ROI activity in months 1–2 is signing local business partners. Their participation makes the platform more valuable for tourists, which in turn makes the platform more valuable for more businesses.

**Save corporate budget for proof.** The LinkedIn and corporate sales channel requires almost no media spend — it requires time and credibility. Invest in good materials (PDF deck, photos from early events) to accelerate the sales cycle from Month 2 onward.

**Reinvest commissions.** As experience bookings and marketplace transactions begin generating commission revenue in months 3–6, a portion should be reinvested into paid social to compound tourist acquisition without drawing further on the initial capital.
